It's pretty simple math: You need 6 good linemen to have a good d-line all game. Both first string DE are on the field for every snap, as they are lined up in all defensive formations. Both starting DTs can also share duties as NT, because when you play 3–4–4 the tackles are not "on the field", thus not needed every snap. Only when you're defending with 5 linemen you need a third tackle to come in as NT, that's why you need one more player as third option on NT and DT... and one more to substitute for DE if your starters are tired.   It helps to look at the manual and the formations section, at the bottom you find how the engine selects the players from your depth chart.   I had all first string d-line-men rotate all d-line-positions in todays game. Agility 4. Strength 5. Vision 6. Positioning  ✔ FIXED ATTRIBUTES (Importance)  Intelligence: High  Essential for reading edge blitzes  Poor INT Tackles = more sacks  Teamwork: Medium  Useful but less critical because Tackles isolate 1-on-1 more often  ✔ EXPERIENCE  High importance — especially for pass protection.  📌 IDEAL TACKLE PROFILE  Footwork 40–50  Agility 35–45  Blocking 40–50  Intelligence 30–45  Experience 4–5★  These are your “edge protectors.”  📘 INTELLIGENCE — DOES IT MATTER FOR OL?  Yes, but it is NOT a primary skill.  ✔ Where it matters most:  Center  Tackle  Guard  Higher Intelligence =  better stunt/blitz recognition  fewer missed assignments  more consistent pass protection  smoother inside run blocking  But you do NOT train it — you only draft/recruit based on it.  📗 TEAMWORK — DOES IT MATTER FOR OL?  Yes — teamwork affects unit cohesion and double-team efficiency.  ✔ Teamwork improves:  run blocking coordination  hand-offs during stunts  double-teams  reducing blown assignments  fewer penalties (minor effect)  ✔ Where it matters most:  Center  Guard  Tackle  High Teamwork OL perform more stable and synchronized.
