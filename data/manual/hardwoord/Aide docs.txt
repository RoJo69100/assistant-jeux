"I think the target goal for prospects differ on a few things. Right now I can think of four factors that plays into how I evaluate and recruit guys and the contacts I give someone. Note: These are general rules because each player is obviously different.

Skill/Star ranking of prospect. I tend to put more contacts into the higher rated players because teams usually go after them pretty seriously.

Location. Since you get a boost for the players in your region, you don't have to put in to many contacts to jump up to lead with a player. I also don't have to open as much of an "interest lead" on an in region player because I can easily put these players to open back up a lead on a guy if I lost it.

Time of commitment A player who is willing to commit early means I just need to be leading the sprint. That means I to put to many contacts in on the guy to see where he commits. The guys who wait to commit usually means for me that I bigger lead, so it is harder for teams who have lost their initial prospects to catch up to me.

Level of competition If a bunch of ownerless division three teams have interest in my player, I tend to spend my contacts elsewhere. The opposite is true for teams with owners especially if there division 1. Also make sure to check later on in the season to see if your competitors ends up running out of scholarships. If they are full, you don't have to worry about the team stealing the player from you. Also, when you recruit HS FR-JR and JUCO FR take now how they finish the year. A promotion can raise the prospect's interest after the season, and a demotion can lower a prospect's interest. Through a relatively small sample size, it seems that a positive effect more than demotion causes a negative effect."

You are describing what condition you want your change in settings to occur.
So in your example If you set an upper threshold of <= 10. With no lower threshold set. Then you are saying "If my opponent scores any number of points in the paint less than or equal to 10 then "

Most of the instructions make more sense to set a lower threshold in my opinion.
"If opponent points in the paint - Lower Threshold >= (some number I decide means they are dominating the paint) then - Set Extend defense to -3"
"If opponent 3-pointers made - Lower Threshold >= 6 Then Set Extend Defense to +2"

Using the lower and upper threshold would be more for things like.
(Threshold Type = Point Differential)
If we're down lots use this tactic (Lower Threshold = None + Upper threshold <= -20)
If we're down a little use this tactic (Lower Threshold >= -19 + Upper threshold <= -1)
If we're up a little use this tactic (Lower Threshold >= 0 + Upper threshold <= 10)
If we're up a lot use this tactic (Lower Threshold >= 11 + Upper threshold = None)
